Less waste, more enjoyment

The proposed Oceania Healthy De-Slop Co-ops connect better everyday living with community capability. “De-slop” means reducing what makes life needlessly confusing, wasteful or unhealthy, while making room for things people enjoy and find useful. It applies to food and wellbeing, information and culture, and the quality of public decision-making.

From growing to a shared table

Food gives this programme a natural beginning. Garden Mate supports growing and observation. Kitchen Mate helps people understand what food they have and what they could cook. Fermentation Mate supports learning about preservation. Event Mate helps turn food and hospitality into well-organised gatherings. Together they follow food from growing and purchasing through cooking, sharing, preservation and the handling of surplus.

A willing household or host kitchen offers one possible beginning. An intelligent conversation about available ingredients, a meal and avoidable waste might lead into growing, a shared table or preservation. Personally chosen phone or sensor observations could help where they reduce effort. Cooks, growers and participants would shape what the tools are useful for. Food-handling knowledge remains important; an AI suggestion alone does not establish that a practice is safe.

C-hours can recognise hosting, mentoring, helping with a community garden, sharing skills, checking information and organising an activity. Outcomes can include food used, people learning a skill and a host’s reduced workload. These records help a neighbouring community adopt an approach that has worked.

Connection, care and health research

The health pathway begins with connection, accessible learning and practical support for living well. People can choose activities involving movement, food, creative expression and reflection. Care is provided according to the relevant service arrangements and people’s needs. A person does not have to contribute hours to deserve it.

More advanced proposals include health cooperatives, Aura Genesis and Aura Geode. Aura Genesis is a proposed voluntary learning and reflection journey supported by a personal Aura. Aura Geode is a proposed immersive physical environment, including a specialist line of hyperbaric research. These require their own clinical, engineering, funding and research development. The Australian ethics framework provides relevant context for developing any human research. [E3]

The ambition includes better support for ageing, carers and people experiencing cognitive difficulties. These are questions for careful development and evidence. Research interests include dementia, rejuvenation, longevity and health, with evidence to be assessed for each specific intervention. Community participation and health research are distinct choices, with medical information remaining a matter of personally controlled consent.

A locally shaped programme across Oceania

Across Oceania, “surge” means a coordinated increase in useful capability: more people able to host, teach, care, build and maintain shared systems. Local communities choose their priorities and work with neighbouring communities when that helps.
