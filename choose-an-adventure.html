Choose your own adventure

Begin with something you enjoy, something you already contribute or something that would make your day easier.

Adapted from the 23 September 2026 plan. The proposed system is open to development with participants.

Begin with your life

“What would you enjoy doing?” offers a starting question. Making and fixing, food and gardens, stories and music, nature and Country, health and connection, or learning and technology are possible doorways. Someone arriving with an entirely different idea might open another.

A first encounter might be as modest as help with something already making life difficult. Someone arriving tired or sceptical brings valuable knowledge about what this idea is missing. The question is what would make their day easier or more enjoyable, including whether this is useful to them at all. Learning the ecosystem, sharing a personal history or taking on extra unpaid work is not the invitation.

A first conversation to try

Here is one possible first encounter to try and reshape with participants. It begins through an intelligent conversation, perhaps reached through a notice board, a story, a friend, a host or a personal Aura. Everyday care and informal contribution belong in this exploration alongside organised activities. The intended experience uses agent interactions rather than enrolment forms or routine timesheets.

Begin with an interest

A conversation about what you enjoy, already contribute or would like to try might lead to an adventure. Useful information would include who is involved, timing, support, costs and whether the work is voluntary or paid.

Explore the information involved

You and the agent would work through what a proposed activity draws on and what you want kept private or shared. A phone or a supported conversation is a starting possibility; a wearable is an optional addition. Participants’ experiences would shape how these conversations become understandable and effortless.

Enjoy taking part

Within the boundaries you choose, the agent might draw on a conversation, activity information or device observations to help recognise the contribution. Where another person’s judgement adds something useful, a host, peer or recipient might help with confirmation. The pilot is a place to discover when that helps and when it becomes needless administration.

Reflect and follow your curiosity

A short conversation might bring together what happened, any correction you want to make and the community’s chosen thanks. Another interest, a continuing project or a break are all ordinary next chapters. The sequence itself is open to improvement.

One afternoon, many possible paths

Consider an illustrative visitor journey. A visitor hears about a shared-table and garden afternoon and asks their agent to help them join. It explains the activity and offers to prepare a contribution record using the agreed session information. Their private location history and health information remain outside that agreement. They help prepare food, learn a preservation technique and meet a filmmaker documenting the project. Afterwards, they check their contribution through a short conversation and choose to help edit a film from home. Another participant follows an interest in garden sensors; another discovers they enjoy hosting. Any recording or publication of their story has its own consent.

These are different paths through the same community experience. The food supports a gathering; the gathering creates relationships; the story invites more people; the observations improve the garden; the records help the next host. Each contribution can build practical capacity, resilience and regeneration. Actual improvements are described and measured where possible, rather than inferred from the hour count.

Make room for fun and quiet contribution

Shared challenges, discoveries, seasonal adventures, skill milestones and celebrations offer playful possibilities. Participants would bring their own ideas about what is enjoyable, meaningful or off-putting. Quiet contribution matters too. The ambition is recognition that adds joy without making available time a measure of someone’s worth.

For tourism, the invitation is to contribute meaningfully to Country and community while enjoying a visit. Local people and visitors would work out what fits the place, the relationships and the capacity available. Skills, friendships and continuing projects might travel home with them.
