- The Risk: The 2ATA, oxygen-enriched environment presents an extreme fire risk. A single spark from a lithium-ion battery can be catastrophic.
- The Blocker: All modern, wireless consumer wearables (e.g., Muse S, Fitbit Versa 2) are explicitly prohibited and unusable . They are banned ignition sources, and their Bluetooth/Wi-Fi signals cannot penetrate the metal/acrylic shell of the HBOT chamber, which functions as a Faraday cage.

1. The "Data Umbilicus": A professionally modified, hermetically-sealed, pressure-rated bulkhead connector (e.g., Lemo, Fischer) that is welded or gasket-sealed to penetrate the chamber shell.
2. The "Internal Sensor Harness": A custom-fabricated, unified sensor harness made from biocompatible (ISO 10993) and fire-retardant, medical-grade materials (e.g., silicone, TPU). This harness will integrate a suite of wired, Tier 2 (Prosumer) or Tier 3 (Clinical-Grade) sensors, such as an OpenBCI Cyton+Daisy (EEG) and Shimmer3 (ECG/GSR) modules.

- The Only Solution: The ecosystem could secure an On-Hire Labour Agreement (OHLA) with the Department of Home Affairs. The OHLA is a special, complex visa
- arrangement that specifically permits an approved sponsor (RSC) to "on-hire" sponsored workers to third-party clients.
- The 12-Month Clock: An OHLA application has an important prerequisite: the business "could be lawfully operating in Australia for at least 12 months" before it can even apply.

This "Visa Bottleneck" dictates a fundamental strategic timeline for the entire ecosystem. 'Ready SET Co.' cannot sponsor skilled migrants in Year 2. The entire Year 2 operational strategy could be to "start the 12-month clock" on Day 1. RSC could operate its domestic labour-hire and CaaS business, meticulously documenting its compliance, its finances, and its extensive Labour Market Testing (LMT). The primary regulatory goal for RSC in Year 2 is to build the "fortress" of evidence required to lodge a successful, non-refusable OHLA application on Day 1 of Year 3. This is an important, immovable constraint on the ecosystem's high-tech growth.
