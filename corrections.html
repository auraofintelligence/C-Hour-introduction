The current interpretation

A C-hour recognises verified voluntary human time and has no monetary equivalent. Communities choose their own thanks and reciprocity. Money, contribution and outcomes remain separately recorded.

Proposed pilot arrangements, facilities, partnerships and intelligent agent capabilities are ideas to develop with people. The source archive does not establish that they are already operating.

Current interpretation and precedence

This planning edition incorporates Luke Nathan Hayes’s explicit clarification of 23 September 2026. C-hours recognise verified voluntary human time. Communities choose their own reciprocity, thanks and rewards. C-hours have no monetary equivalent and are not money, tradeable financial assets, wage substitutes or financial claims. The social transformation is framed through Mutual Futures, Try Everything Once, just-in-time learning, Intermittent Retirement, Universal Intelligence and Joyful Responsible Abundance. Do not replace that architecture with a mass job replacement narrative. Money, contribution and outcomes remain separately recorded.

The earlier audit was too broad where it treated all rewards, reciprocal help or references to non-financial recognition as monetisation. Preserve community choice and genuine reciprocity. Correct language that creates a fixed conversion, priced entitlement, financial backing, compulsory score or universal promise of redemption. Assess the mechanism in context, rather than treating individual words as conclusive.

Use the newest explicit author instructions first, then this corrected planning edition, then source material consistent with it. An older passage does not become authoritative because several documents repeat it. The following references identify affected supplied versions, not every future revision of a similarly named file. Historical filenames are retained verbatim so the affected files can be found; use Ready S.E.T. Co-op or Ready Sustainable Employment and Training Cooperative in new writing. PDF references are file page numbers; Markdown line numbers refer to the uploaded copies reviewed for this plan. Quotations below are error locators, not instructions to implement.

Direct monetisation and financial substitution to remove

B1. 04-Oceania_Health_and_AI_Surge_Plan.md, lines 135 to 145 and 225. Remove “dual-currency system”, “monetise civic contribution”, instant service redemption and “Earn-to-Heal” as financial or qualifying mechanisms. Preserve the connected health, food, AI and civic programme, with freely chosen community recognition.

B2. Ready SET Co. Business Strategy Development(20260919-033618).pdf, page 19. Remove the statement that C-hours “will be monetized”, the proposed C-hour fund paying people for their recorded hours, and conversion into commercial discounts. Rewrite around separately funded activities, paid work where appropriate and locally chosen non-monetised thanks.

B3. Straddie Employment and Training Company (SETCo) S...(20260919-033619).pdf, pages 11 to 12, sections 4.4 and 4.5. Remove the instruction to set a “fair market value” in AUD for one C-hour, record an “AUD-equivalent” and treat C-hours as remuneration. Any legal analysis premised on those invented features needs reassessment against the actual proposed activity.

B4. SETCo Business Setup and Compliance(1).pdf, pages 7, 22, 35 and 38 to 39. Remove C-hour wages, payslip integration, payment for training and nominal or redemption-based monetary valuation. Preserve ordinary financial administration for real money and separately assess particular material rewards where necessary.

B5. SETCo 2026 Pitch Plan Revision(20260919-033618).pdf, pages 9 to 10; Ready SET Co. Year 2 Strategic Plan(20260919-033618).pdf, pages 9 to 10. Remove the wealth fund as a C-hour “Reserve Bank”, convertibility and “circulating currency”. Retain cooperative development, contribution records and community recognition.

B6. Political Ethics, Technology, and Australian Law(20260919-033841).pdf, page 2; Spec-Driven AI Governance Architecture Australia(3).pdf, page 11; Local Government 2Funding Inquiry Submission(20260917-135841).pdf, pages 2 to 3. This register concerns the supplied submission file, not a verified correction of any public parliamentary record.

Financial backing and redemption claims to rewrite

B7. Unpaid Work, C-Hours, Societal Costs(1).pdf, pages 2 and 11; Legislative Strategy for Regenerative Assets(20260919-033619).pdf, page 8; Virtual Minjerribah: Time Simulations & Data(20260919-024742).pdf, page 3. Remove monetary underwriting and guaranteed redemption value. Community assets can support activities without backing the C-hour as a financial instrument.

B8. Australian C-Hour Legislative Strategy(20260919-004710).pdf, pages 10 and 12. Reassess the assumed goods-and-services redemption and transactional token system. Do not carry its legal conclusions into the corrected concept without examining the actual design and current law. Non-monetary recognition does not by itself establish a tax exemption or require a particular statutory carve-out.

B9. Australian Community Project Rollout Plan.pdf, page 6; Local Islands Narrative Strategy(20260919-025101).pdf, pages 6 to 7; Shared Table Initiative(20260919-025101).pdf, page 4. Remove “purchasing power”, liquidity claims and promised conversion into tickets, rate discounts, ferry vouchers, produce or wellness services. Retain the activities and scope for communities to choose thanks and reciprocal opportunities without pricing hours.

B10. Queenslands Heart-First Ai Surge Plan(20260917-135739).pdf, page 6; Australia's Olympic Space Gambit(20260919-024829).pdf, pages 2, 5, 7 and 11; GAJRA Earth-Space-AI Summit(20260919-024829).pdf, page 4. Remove C-hour payment for health services, financial underwriting and automatic exchange into housing, transport or treatment. Retain the ambitions for shared capability, research and cooperation as proposals.

B11. 05-Health-Tech_Cooperative_Business_Plan.md, line 119, sections 5.0 and 5.2, and Table 3; related Health-Tech Cooperative Business Plan(20260919-004710).pdf, especially pages 16 and 19. Remove C-hours as compensation, a financial asset class or a regenerative finance mechanism. Preserve separately financed cooperative ownership and voluntary contribution. Reciprocity language requiring clarification rather than blanket deletion

B12. 01-Sovereign_Space_Builder__Full_System_Specification_-2-.md, line 245, section 3.3; Sovereign Space Builder (part 1).pdf, page 9; Sovereign Space Builder part 2 Full System Specification(20260919-004709).pdf, pages 12 and 31. “Time-bank token” and exchange-for-help language should be updated to community-chosen reciprocity and recognised contribution. Preserve mutual help, enjoyable challenges and recognition; do not assume a fixed exchange rate or debt of equivalent service.

B13. Virtual Minjerribah Design Document.pdf, page 13; Web3 Sensorium for Science Debate(20260919-004710).pdf, page 24; Generative Simulation Engine & Asset Pipeline for Quandamooka Country.pdf, page 12. Clarify “time-bank currency”, redeemable resource credits and time-banking references. Preserve learning, skills records, verifiable contribution and locally chosen opportunities. Resource access needs its own stated allocation arrangements; it is not automatically purchased with C-hours.

Everyday contribution and updated scale

The supplied Markdown versions Unpaid_Work,_C-Hours,_Societal_Costs.md and Australian_C-Hour_Legislative_Strategy.md retain useful background as well as superseded assumptions. Preserve the unpaid-work paper’s scale argument, but use the 2024 ABS release for current Australian time-use context. Keep economic estimates of societal costs distinct from C-hour valuation. Do not multiply participant averages by the entire population, treat overlapping care as extra elapsed hours, or claim that recording contribution will eliminate the costs of mental illness, crime or demographic change.

Everyday informal contribution belongs within the intended scope, including household and family care. Do not narrow eligibility to registered organisations or hosted events. Simple, proportionate confirmation methods need to be developed with participants. Elders’ own life experience and authority are not dependent on a generic permission requirement; specific questions about recording, publishing or restricted collective knowledge should be treated separately.

Agent interactions and the Sensorium clarification

P3 records the author’s latest design direction. Earlier planning edition 2, Chapters 3 and 4, framed onboarding and recording around hosted steps, paper records, templates and periodic manual review. Chapter 6 put manual entries before intelligent assistance. Those passages are superseded by this edition. Do not reintroduce enrolment forms, routine timesheets or compulsory host signatures as the intended participant experience.

Intelligent agent interactions, phones, optional smart wearables and edge networks remain the author’s design direction, alongside personally controllable consent and human verification where appropriate. These are starting foundations for exploration with participants. Detailed interactions, record structures, norms and workflows remain open to revision through pilots; do not present them as established rules or granted permissions.

L0 names the private citizen and their controlled private environment. L0 to L3 is an initial illustration, with further levels and overlapping arrangements left open. Wider participation does not imply surrender of personal sovereignty. The practical expression of that principle is design work to undertake with people, not authority conferred by this document.

Avoid turning a provisional idea into authority

The author’s latest clarification governs the tone as well as the mechanics. This is a malleable thought experiment seeking input from real people as it becomes practical through pilots. Earlier editions used declarative record schemas, universal workflows and phrases such as “people can” or “the system should” in ways that sounded like permissions handed down by a central authority. Treat those passages as superseded design sketches. Future writing should identify whose idea is being explored, invite alternatives and leave genuine room for participants to change it. Preserve the author’s explicit non-monetary framing and personal sovereignty without inventing powers to regulate other people. Remember the circumstances of the people being invited: stress, overwork, fear, frustration, scarce time and money, and experiences of mistreatment by systems. Do not ask them to accept a declaration of trustworthiness. Frame trust as something earned through useful help, lower burdens, honest limits and responsiveness to their experience.

Other claims that need a different evidential status

The older Oceania surge and health-tech drafts include enormous deployment numbers, guaranteed savings and strong health claims. Treat these as valid thought experiment scenarios or research ambitions unless separately supported. Do not present budgets, devices, facilities, partnerships, clinical outcomes or political events as secured by repetition across the archive.

The newer Queens Venture Capital Planning Document, page 10; 82 Claytons Road Anglican Partnership Proposal, page 8; and UNGA81 Joyful Responsible Abundance Refined New Reference Links, page 6, provide clearer non-monetary C-hour framing. They remain subject to the latest community-rewards clarification and their own proposal status. Their references to older documents do not reinstate superseded mechanisms.

When updating any source, preserve its useful purpose, change the affected mechanism and record the amendment date. Keep the superseded original in an archive. Do not silently rewrite historical submissions or imply that an external organisation has accepted a correction. For future AI work, provide this register alongside selected sources and require conflicting passages to be identified rather than blended into a new definition.
