The person behind the invitation

Luke Nathan Hayes is based in Amity on Minjerribah and is developing Aura of Intelligence and a collection of connected projects in community participation, media, AI, health research and regenerative enterprise. The breadth has a common purpose: help people understand themselves, choose their direction and build Joyful Responsible Abundance together.

An invitation people can reshape

Joyful Responsible Abundance means a life worth enjoying, responsibility for our effects on others and useful capacity that more people can share. A shared meal, a film afternoon, a garden project or an introduction to AI could be a worthwhile beginning. Further interests might lead towards community science, cooperative enterprise or collaboration across Oceania.

This is a malleable thought experiment becoming practical through proposed pilots. An unexpected use, an objection or a better idea may change the design. Participation is also authorship: people bring their own knowledge of what helps, what is missing and what creates unnecessary effort.

Someone arriving stressed, sceptical, overworked or short of money does not owe this idea their trust or another unpaid commitment. Trust would have to grow through useful experiences, honest responses and changes made because people spoke up. The pilot, partnerships, facilities and growth stages remain proposals unless specifically evidenced otherwise.

Personal tools and shared purpose

Aura of Intelligence is the proposed personal layer. It lets a person organise a digital representation of their body, mind, interests, knowledge and intentions, with control over what they keep private and what they share. GAJRA Earth, the Global Association for Joyful Responsible Abundance on Earth, is the proposed association network through which people and communities can collaborate. At this stage, GAJRA Earth is an unregistered concept, not an established global institution.

These projects begin with Luke’s ideas and experiments. Other people are invited to choose, challenge, adapt and add their own. The idea is a grassroots patchwork, with each community contributing something distinctive. Compatible tools and shared learning might connect those pieces while leaving plenty of room for different worldviews and ways of doing things.

Begin with real relationships

Minjerribah offers a place to begin with real relationships and real needs. The proposed pilot connects the island with willing organisations across Redlands and the Oodgeroo and Bowman areas. Luke reports that, at his meeting with the state member for Oodgeroo, she expressed interest in connecting him with volunteer organisations to discuss a pilot. That is an opening for conversations, not a confirmed partnership or government commitment.

On Quandamooka Country, the people whose knowledge, stories, places and practices are involved bring their own authority and relationships to the conversation. The invitation is to develop activities with them, taking their lead about what they wish to share. This plan does not speak for them or confer permission on their behalf.

There are many ways to begin

Our invitation is broad. A young person might begin through music or games. A visitor might join a local project during their holiday. A carer might share practical experience. A programmer might help a club organise its records. An organisation might host an activity it already wants to run. Everyone needs an understandable first step and a reason to come back.
